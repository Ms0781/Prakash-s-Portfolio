02 · The Challenge

Navigating High-Stress Air Travel Friction

Air travel is one of the most cognitively taxing consumer environments: hurried gate changes, confusing terminal layouts, broken public Wi-Fi, and fragmented airline apps. FlyFi was conceived to consolidate all flight telemetry and terminal connectivity into a single, reliable companion.

The Travel Friction

Fragmented Airport Chaos

  • ✕ Delayed PA Announcements: Travelers learn about gate changes or cancellations after crowds have already overwhelmed the customer service desk.
  • ✕ Unreliable Terminal Wi-Fi: Landing in an international airport with no cellular roaming leaves travelers unable to access rideshare or hotel confirmations.
  • ✕ Cluttered In-Flight Portals: Trying to understand airline in-flight Wi-Fi pricing and connection steps at 35,000 feet causes passenger frustration.
  • ✕ Poor Single-Handed Usability: Most flight apps require two hands to operate, creating friction when passengers are dragging rollaboard bags.
The FlyFi Solution

Consolidated Flight Intelligence

  • ✓ Real-Time Gate & Carousel Alerts: Instant push notifications broadcast gate reassignment and baggage carousel numbers ahead of terminal boards.
  • ✓ Offline Airport Wi-Fi Keychain: Verified passwords and captive-portal bypass guides for 500+ global hubs, pre-cached for offline access.
  • ✓ In-Flight Satellite Guides: Clear instructions to connect to Viasat, Starlink, and Gogo in-flight networks across 30+ major airlines.
  • ✓ Thumb-Zone Ergonomics: Bottom action drawers and tactile numeric keypads designed specifically for seamless single-handed navigation.
03 · Interaction Design

Designing for High-Stress Airport Ergonomics

Every interface decision in FlyFi was guided by real-world airport ergonomics: navigating crowded concourses, single-handed phone usage while wheeling luggage, and readability under variable terminal lighting conditions:

Principle 01

The "Luggage in Hand" Rule

Primary controls—flight tracking bottom sheets, search inputs, and gate refresh buttons—are strictly anchored in the lower 40% of the screen within comfortable thumb reach.

Principle 02

Direct Sunlight Glare Protection

Airport terminals feature vast floor-to-ceiling glass concourses. Card borders are treated with 1px distinct outlines, deep navy text (#0B132B), and high-contrast status pills for instant readability.

Principle 03

Offline-First Caching

Flight itineraries, Wi-Fi passwords, and terminal maps automatically cache on device upon departure, ensuring zero data loss when travelers disembark abroad without roaming data.

07 · Design System

Figma Atomic Design Tokens & Component System

Every screen in FlyFi was established around atomic UI tokens defined in Figma (Frame 1): prioritizing high-contrast legibility in bright airport concourses, tactile pill action buttons, and clear color semantics for flight statuses.

  • Corner Radius 16px
  • Inner Padding 20px
  • Elevation Shadow 0 8px 24px (Soft)
  • Card Border 1px Light Contrast

09 · Retrospective

Design Retrospective & Travel UX Learnings

Designing a flight utility app with offline capabilities revealed key insights regarding traveler behavior and interface resilience:

Takeaway 01

Latency Is Traveler Anxiety

In travel apps, interface delays trigger immediate panic. Ensuring that bottom sheets (`03 full popup.png`) render locally without network dependencies proved crucial during dead zones inside jet bridges.

Takeaway 02

Offline-First Is Non-Negotiable

International travelers often experience 15 to 45 minutes of zero connectivity after touchdown before clearing customs. Caching terminal Wi-Fi passwords and baggage carousel info locally is what made the app indispensable.

Takeaway 03

Restraint in Dense Aviation Data

Pilots and air traffic systems generate endless telemetry. Designing for consumer travelers meant prioritizing only 3 core numbers on the HUD: flight status, gate number, and remaining flight time.
